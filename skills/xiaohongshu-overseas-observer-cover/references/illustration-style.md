# Illustration Style

## How to use references

Use supplied images to identify transferable visual attributes: line quality, palette restraint, paper texture, character proportions, negative space, and scene density. Do not reproduce a signature, watermark, exact character, exact outfit, exact pose, or exact composition. Do not ask an image model to imitate a named living artist.

## Core style

A unified retro storybook character system:

- thin black or dark-brown ink outlines with small hand-drawn irregularities
- simplified round faces, dot-like eyes, restrained blush, and minimal facial detail
- slightly oversized heads, slim limbs, compact hands and feet, and readable silhouettes
- vintage-inspired children’s or student clothing without copying a specific reference outfit
- flat muted color fields with subtle colored-pencil or watercolor grain
- simple stage-like composition with large areas of unbroken background
- gentle visual wit: animals behaving like companions, playful scale, costume metaphors, or a small narrative action

The result should feel printed and hand-drawn, not digitally glossy. Preserve a little unevenness in the ink and color fill, but keep anatomy and object construction coherent.

## Preferred palette

Use four to six colors per illustration:

- brick red
- cream
- caramel brown
- dusty blue
- olive green
- muted pink
- black or charcoal for accents

Avoid bright digital gradients and high-gloss highlights.

## Composition for this cover

- panel size: usually 1080×520
- background: one flat muted color
- main group: centered horizontally, positioned in the lower 68–78% of the panel
- empty top safety band: at least 60 px, visibly free of hair, ears, balloons, props, and linework
- focal figure or group occupies about 42–65% of panel height
- props remain close to the character rather than scattered across the entire panel
- use one focal action; avoid a crowded montage
- keep all important content inside the central 82% of the width so later fitting does not crop it

## Scene grammar

Choose the smallest cast that expresses the topic:

- solo reflection: one student, one action, one or two props, optional animal;
- social adjustment: two to four figures or a human-and-animal group with clear interaction;
- academic work: desk, books, notes, tea, lamp, or clock shapes without letters;
- emotional metaphor: balloons, oversized food or stationery, animal companions, or costume-like details;
- supervisor meeting: waiting, note-taking, listening, or walking toward a door represented without signs or readable labels.

Across consecutive covers, vary at least three of: cast size, pose, clothing color, animal species, prop family, and panel color.

## Character ideas

- student in a brick-red coat holding books
- student in a cream dress walking among geese
- two students sharing tea and notes at a small table
- student waiting with a notebook while a cat rests nearby
- small group with rabbit companions or balloon-like metaphors
- vintage suitcase decorated only with abstract shapes, never city names
- cups, books, folders, clocks, or stationery without lettering

## Negative constraints

- no anime eyes
- no photorealism
- no 3D rendering
- no cinematic lighting
- no modern vector corporate style
- no complex campus building
- no readable text or logos
- no signature or watermark
- no subject touching or crossing the top edge
- no copied character, outfit, pose, or composition from a supplied reference
- no repeated default scene across multiple topics
